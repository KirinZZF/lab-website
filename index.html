Wind turbines operate for decades in turbulent wind, ice and salt spray. Component degradation develops over weeks to months before it ends in a sudden, costly stoppage, often offshore or on a remote ridge where repairs need cranes, vessels and weather windows. Every turbine already records its own behaviour through SCADA. The open question is how to turn that record into early, trustworthy and actionable knowledge.

Our aim is condition monitoring that explains why a turbine behaves as it does, predicts what happens next, and evaluates what would happen if it were operated differently.
  • Quantum machine learning for energy and power systems
  • Early, unsupervised fault detection from routine operating data
  • Component-level diagnosis and root-cause tracing grounded in turbine physics
  • Calibrated failure probabilities and remaining useful life
  • Action-conditioned world models that support operating decisions

Featured project

TurbineCore

TurbineCore is an autonomous condition monitoring system. Every day, for every turbine, it reads the machine's own SCADA record, works out how healthy it is, and decides how hard it should be allowed to run, without an engineer in the analysis loop. A world model shared across five wind farms forecasts every channel under a given operator command; on top of it the system detects anomalies, names the faulty component, estimates failure probability over the coming weeks, writes the day up, and learns a power-limit policy that trades energy against failure risk.
